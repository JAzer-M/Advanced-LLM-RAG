They experimented with four learning strategies under three evaluation settings: IN-DOMAIN, OUT-OF-DOMAIN, and CROSS-DATASET, evaluated mainly on their multi-domain MULTI-SUM dataset.

The three settings allow explicitly evaluating models both on the quality of domain-aware text representation and on their adaptation ability in unfamiliar domains [1]. The experiments are performed mainly on the MULTI-SUM dataset, where the source domains are the first five domains in Table 1 (IN-DOMAIN) and the other domains (OUT-OF-DOMAIN) are totally invisible during training; the IN-DOMAIN setting tests the model's ability to learn different domain distributions on a multi-domain set, and OUT-OF-DOMAIN investigates how models perform on unseen domains [2]. The CROSS-DATASET setting evaluates transferability across different datasets (e.g., CNN/DM) rather than only among the domains of MULTI-SUM [7][9]. The four learning strategies investigated were ModelI Basic, ModelII BERT, ModelIII Tag, and ModelIV Meta [9], examined for their ability to deal with the domain shift problem under these three settings [3][8].
