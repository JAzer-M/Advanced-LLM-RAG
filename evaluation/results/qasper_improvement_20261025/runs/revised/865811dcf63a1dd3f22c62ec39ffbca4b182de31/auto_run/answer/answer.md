The analyzed most discriminating patterns are the linguistic/lexico-syntactic patterns learned for FACTUAL and FEELING arguments, notably those involving topic-specific terminology, explanatory language, argument phrases, speaker beliefs/claims, evaluations, and prevalent NP-Prep constructions.

The paper analyzes the most discriminating patterns to understand the defining characteristics of factual and emotional arguments [1]. Patterns associated with factual arguments often include topic-specific terminology, explanatory language, and argument phrases, whereas feeling-based argument patterns are often based on the speaker's own beliefs or claims, or involve assessment or evaluations of the arguments [7]. The analysis further examines the NP Prep patterns as the most prevalent construction type, with patterns containing the preposition "of" making up the vast majority of prepositional phrases for both classes, though more common in the FACT class [10]. The derived characteristic syntactic forms and distinctions between technical and more opinionated terminologies are used to discriminate between the classes [2].
