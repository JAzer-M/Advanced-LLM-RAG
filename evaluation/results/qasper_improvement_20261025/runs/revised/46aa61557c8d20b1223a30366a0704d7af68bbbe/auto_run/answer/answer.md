Sentence alignment quality is evaluated by a manual human evaluation using a 5-point scale from Kocabiyikoglu et al. (2018), with two annotators rating items and inter-annotator agreement measured by Krippendorff’s α-reliability score (0.77 for text-to-text alignment).

The 5-point scale ranges from 1 (wrong alignment) to 5 (correct alignment and fully compositional translation) [4]. Two annotators each rated 30 items per bin, with 10 shared items to calculate inter-annotator reliability [4]. The agreement was measured using Krippendorff’s α-reliability score for ordinal ratings, scoring 0.77 for text-to-text alignment quality [7]. Additionally, text alignment quality can be assessed (and the corpus filtered) using hunalign confidence scores, which bin alignments into low, moderate, and high quality ranges [1][8].
