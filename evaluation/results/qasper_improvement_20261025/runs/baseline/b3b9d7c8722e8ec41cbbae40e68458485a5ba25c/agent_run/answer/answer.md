Speech (audio-text) alignment quality is evaluated through a manual evaluation using a 3-point scale: 1) wrong alignment, 2) partial alignment (some words or sentences may be missing), and 3) correct alignment, allowing non-spoken syllables at the start or end [1]. Two annotators each rated 30 items from each bin, with 10 items shared between them to enable measuring inter-annotator reliability [1]. Inter-annotator agreement was measured using Krippendorff's α-reliability score for ordinal ratings, which reached 1.00 for audio-text alignment quality ratings [8]. The results were averaged over 90 items and two raters [7], and the audio-text alignment was rated as being of generally high quality [9].
