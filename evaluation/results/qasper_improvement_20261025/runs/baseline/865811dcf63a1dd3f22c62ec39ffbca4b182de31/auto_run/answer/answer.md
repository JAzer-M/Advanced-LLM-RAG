The paper analyzes the most discriminating patterns to understand the defining characteristics of factual and emotional arguments [1]. For FACTUAL arguments, patterns with prepositional phrases (especially NP Prep) and passive voice verb phrases are more common (e.g., NP Prep instantiations FC1, FC5, FC8, FC10; PassVP instantiations FC2 and FC4) [2]. For FEELING arguments, expressions with adjectives and active voice verb phrases are more common [2]. Additionally, factual argument patterns often include topic-specific terminology, explanatory language, and argument phrases, while feeling-based argument patterns often reflect the speaker's own beliefs or claims, or involve assessment or evaluation of the arguments [5].
