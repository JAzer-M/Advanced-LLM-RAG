Beyond privacy, the paper highlights **social impact and biases** emanating from clinical notes and their processing as a major ethical challenge [1].

Specific aspects discussed include:

- **Bias in documentation**: Clinical records can reflect clinician's bias (more serious illness may be more accurately documented due to increased attention), patient's recall bias, suppression of information for stigmatized diseases, and distorted documentation (e.g., traffic injuries recorded inaccurately to avoid legal consequences) [10].
- **Health disparities**: Clinical notes may reflect health disparities [10].
- **Group-level harms**: While sanitization focuses on protecting the individual, ethical harms are still possible at the group level [4].
- **Annotated data scarcity due to ethics constraints**: Annotation projects are typically constrained to a single healthcare organization, so much annotation effort is lost because data cannot be shared with the wider research community [5].
- **Social media use**: Using social media data (e.g., linking online health information to health records) requires ethical considerations similar to those in the clinical domain [6].
